# Alt text for the social figures

Paste the matching block into the image's alt-text field when posting.
Bluesky enforces a 1 MB upload cap; `square/` holds 1080x1080 JPEGs
under that limit, and the PNGs in this directory are the full-size
originals for LinkedIn and the portfolio. The GIFs are already under
the cap and are posted as-is.

## before_after.png

Two satellite maps of the Santa Monica Mountains coastline side by side. Left, 15 December 2024: vegetation index shown in green across the hills, with a brown scar inland of Pepperdine University labelled as the Franklin Fire, which burned 9-18 December. Right, 23 January 2025: burn severity for the same area, with a large orange-red patch covering the eastern hills between Saddle Peak and Las Flores Canyon where the Palisades Fire burned, and blue elsewhere.

## char_fraction.png

Map of the Franklin Fire scar in Malibu Canyon, imaged 15 December 2024. Colour shows the fraction of each 30-metre pixel made of char, from dark (none) through orange to white (all char), derived by spectral unmixing. A bright white and orange burn scar fills the centre of the frame north of Pepperdine University.

## composites.png

Three renderings of the same Tanager-1 scene over the Palisades burn area, each built from three of its 426 bands. Left, natural colour: grey-green hillsides. Centre, near-infrared: living vegetation glows bright red and the burn scar reads dark. Right, shortwave infrared: the burn scar turns orange and tan against blue unburned terrain, and the fire's edge becomes a hard line.

## hero_severity.png

Burn severity map of the western Palisades Fire footprint from Planet Tanager-1. Blue marks unburned ground across the western Santa Monica Mountains near Malibu and Pepperdine University; a dense orange-red area covers the eastern third of the frame from Saddle Peak down to Las Flores Canyon. Small white squares scattered through the red area mark 984 structures CAL FIRE inspectors recorded as destroyed. An inset locates the scene in Southern California.

## hypercube.png

A hyperspectral data cube drawn in three dimensions. The top face is a natural-colour satellite image of the Santa Monica Mountains. The front and right faces glow in purple, orange and yellow, showing reflectance across all 426 spectral bands from 376 to 2499 nanometres. Two solid black horizontal stripes cross both faces where atmospheric water vapour blocks the signal.

## sensor_comparison.png

Horizontal bar chart. Agreement with Tanager-1's char fraction map after resampling the same pixels down to each sensor's bands: Tanager-1 with 426 bands scores 1.000, EMIT with 285 bands 0.991, PRISMA with 239 bands 0.957, and Sentinel-2 with 10 bands only 0.361. An arrow spans the gap between Sentinel-2 and the hyperspectral sensors.

## spectral_signatures.png

Line chart of surface reflectance against wavelength from 380 to 2500 nanometres for three surfaces measured by Tanager-1. Living chaparral in green dips at 672 nanometres for chlorophyll, jumps sharply at the red edge, peaks near 1120 nanometres and falls away through the shortwave infrared. Burned ground in red starts low and flat and rises steadily to overtake vegetation past 1500 nanometres. Bare soil in orange rises smoothly throughout. A bar beneath the chart contrasts Tanager-1's single continuous 426-band span against Sentinel-2's ten separate narrow blocks.

## structures.png

Left, a burn severity map with 2,046 CAL FIRE structure inspections plotted as coloured dots, red for destroyed and blue for undamaged, clustered along the canyons of the eastern burn area. Right, the distribution of satellite-measured severity for each damage class: destroyed structures centre near dNBR 0.37 and undamaged ones near 0.23, with the two distributions clearly overlapping.

## water_content.png

Map of canopy water content across north Los Angeles County on 7 April 2025, from the depth of the 970-nanometre water absorption feature. Blue areas hold more water in the vegetation; brown ridges and drainages are drier. A note records that the values are relative and not calibrated against field fuel-moisture measurements.

## hughes_recovery.gif

Two-frame animation of the Hughes Fire footprint near Castaic Lake that cuts between 23 January 2025 and 7 April 2025. The first frame is mostly bare brown burned ground. Seventy-four days later the same hills are broadly green, showing vegetation that has regrown, with mean Normalized Burn Ratio rising from 0.017 to 0.192.

## palisades_before_after.gif

Two-frame animation of the Santa Monica Mountains that cuts between 15 December 2024 and 23 January 2025. In the first frame the hills inside a yellow outline are green with living chaparral. In the second frame everything inside that outline has turned dark brown, marking where the Palisades Fire burned. The outline is the official NIFC fire perimeter and it matches the brown area closely.
